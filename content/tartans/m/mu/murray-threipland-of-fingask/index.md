Its design is pattern [RBYGRBWW](/stripes/rbygrbww/) — the page of every tartan sharing this colour sequence.

Recorded in the Scottish Register of Tartans as 'Drummond of Fingask', but there is no separate Drummond of Fingask line — this is a mis-attribution. The Fingask tartans belong to the Murray-Threipland family of Fingask Castle in the Carse of Gowrie, Perthshire. Their treasured relic of the '45 is a length of tartan said to have been cut from a cloak that Prince Charles Edward Stuart left at Fingask after staying there during the 1745 rising; its sett is near-identical to Drummond of Perth. The family is best thought of as keeping that sample rather than as a Drummond cadet branch.

The **Murray Threipland of Fingask** tartan is recorded as a single sett.
