## Provenance

<figure class="logan-scan">
<a href="/sources/logan-1831/setts/macleod.png"><img src="/sources/logan-1831/setts/macleod.png" alt="MacLeod: the source's printed listing, scanned"></a>
<figcaption>Logan, The Scottish Gaël (1831), vol. II p. 406 — page-scan crop (624,899)–(878,1396)</figcaption>
</figure>

James Logan recorded the **MacLeod** sett in 1831, on page 406 of the *Table of Clan Tartans* in *The Scottish Gaël* — the earliest systematic published collection of clan setts. Logan gives the stripe widths in eighths of an inch, measured across the cloth and reflected about each end (a half-sett):

> 1 yellow · ½ black · 6 blue · 6 black · 6 green · ½ black · 2 red · ½ black · 6 green · 6 black · 6 blue · ½ black · 2 yellow

Rendered at 8 threads to the eighth-inch that is `Y/8 K4 B48 K48 G48 K4 R16 K4 G48 K48 B48 K4 Y/16` — the eighths are the captured data, and the threadcount is derived from them at that stated factor. How many threads an eighth of cloth held depends on the weave's density, so the factor is a display calibration, not Logan's count; the sett's identity lives in the proportions, which the eighths record directly. Logan named his colours rather than dyeing to a standard, so the palette here is the Dictionary's modern reading of his names.

See [Logan's Scottish Gaël](/posts/logans-scottish-gael/) for the full table and method.
