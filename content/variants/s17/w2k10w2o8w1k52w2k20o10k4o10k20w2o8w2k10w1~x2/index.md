## Provenance

<figure class="logan-scan">
<a href="/sources/logan-1831/setts/clergy.png"><img src="/sources/logan-1831/setts/clergy.png" alt="Clergy: Logan's printed table listing, scanned"></a>

James Logan recorded the **Clergy** sett in 1831, on page 408 of the *Table of Clan Tartans* in *The Scottish Gaël* — the earliest systematic published collection of clan setts. Logan gives the stripe widths in eighths of an inch, measured across the cloth and reflected about each end (a half-sett):

> ¼ white · 2½ black · ½ white · 2 grey · ½ white · 5 black · 2½ grey · 1 black · 2½ grey · 5 black · ½ white · 13 black · ¼ white · 2 grey · ½ white · 2½ black · ½ white

In threads (at 8 to the eighth-inch) that is `W/2 K20 W4 N16 W4 K40 N20 K8 N20 K40 W4 K104 W2 N16 W4 K20 W/4`. Logan named his colours rather than dyeing to a standard, so the palette here is the Dictionary's modern reading of his names.

See [Logan's Scottish Gaël](/posts/logans-scottish-gael/) for the full table and method.
